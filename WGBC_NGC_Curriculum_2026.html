Teaching Philosophy

The convictions that shape every lesson, every quarter, every year at WGBC NGC.

1 Scripture Is Primary

We are not teaching values with Bible illustrations attached. We are teaching what God has revealed. Every lesson flows from a text. The Big Truth comes from the text — not from a topic chosen first, with verses added for support. Teachers should read the passage with students, not merely reference it.

2 The Goal Is Revealing God and the Gospel

Children should leave knowing more about God — His character, His purposes, His work through Christ. The metric of success is not "did they enjoy it?" but "do they know God better?" A lesson that entertains but does not reveal God has missed the point.

3 Jesus Must Emerge, Not Be Added

Every lesson includes a Christ connection — not as an afterthought, but as the natural destination of the text. Jesus is the fulfilment of every promise, the answer to every problem, and the hero of every narrative. When we teach Abraham, we are teaching about One who would come through his line. When we teach David, we are pointing to a greater King.

4 The Bible Is One Story

One of the most important discoveries any child or teenager can make is that the Bible is not a collection of moral fables. It is one story — God's story, our story, Jesus' story — moving from Creation to Fall to Promise to Redemption to New Creation. The curriculum is designed to build that awareness across every age group.

5 Rich Theology, Simply Explained

We do not need shallow lessons. Children can handle big truths if explained simply. The mistake many churches make is reducing theology to moralism ("Be brave like David") or avoiding deep truth entirely. A six-year-old can understand substitutionary atonement — not in that language, but in those categories.

6 Creative Sundays Reinforce Truth — Not Merely Entertain

Every Creative Sunday has a learning anchor. The activity is designed to reinforce the quarter's central truth through hands-on engagement. If an activity is fun but does not connect to truth, it should be replaced. Activities exist to make truth stick — not to reward attendance.

Weekly Lesson Structure

Every teaching Sunday follows the same rhythm. This trains students to expect Scripture, understand it, and respond to it.

Children & Preteens — Teaching Sunday

1
Opening Worship / Prayer — Set the atmosphere for encountering God, not just learning content.
2
Bible Reading — Read the whole story or passage aloud. Students should hear the actual Word, not just a summary.
3
Teaching — Big truths explained simply. God-revealing truth from the text, not moral lessons.
4
Discussion & Questions — Age-appropriate reflection. Help them think, not just listen.
5
Application Activity — Reinforces the truth through action. Every activity has a learning anchor.
6
Memory Verse / Response Prayer — Scripture goes in. A response comes out. Repetition over months forms theology.

Teenagers — Teaching Sunday

1
Read the Text — Whole passage read together. Not a summary — the actual Scripture.
2
Teaching (45 min) — Exegetical, progressive, Christ-revealing. Don't rush past the text.
3
Christ Revealed — Explicitly show where and how the text points to Jesus.
4
New Creation Reality — What does this mean for the believer in Christ today?
5
Discussion (25 min) — Teens should be challenged to think, not just receive. Hard questions welcome.
6
Application — Specific and actionable. One clear response to the text this week.

On Creative Sundays

Creative Sundays fall on the last Sunday of each month. They are not "off weeks." They are consolidation weeks — where truth taught over the month is reinforced through creative engagement. Every activity in this curriculum has a Scripture anchor. Teachers should be able to articulate exactly which truth the activity is designed to embed. If they cannot, the activity should be replaced or redesigned.
